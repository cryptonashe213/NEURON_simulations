## Full thesis in Spanish

[Read the thesis online (PDF)](https://cryptonashe213.github.io/NEURON_simulations/TFG_Rub%C3%A9n_final.pdf)

This thesis investigates neuronal synchronization in the hippocampus using pairs of neurons coupled through reciprocal somatic inhibition. Simulations were performed in NEURON with biophysical models of increasing morphological complexity: single-compartment neurons, a soma with four dendrites (BallStick4), branched tree models, and a reconstructed hippocampal CA1 interneuron.

Two stimulation protocols were compared: constant current injection (IClamp) and periodic excitatory synaptic input (NetStim). Synchronization was assessed through a spike-phase correlation measure, together with the firing rates of both neurons. The simulations explored the effects of inhibitory strength, stimulation frequency or current amplitude, and the location of excitatory inputs. An extended tree model with six lateral branches was also used to examine how different synaptic distances from the soma affect coordination.

The results show that phase coordination and firing rate provide complementary information. Neurons can remain synchronized while firing below the input frequency, and similar firing rates do not necessarily imply simultaneous spikes. The effects of inhibition depend on the stimulation protocol and neuronal configuration. In the reconstructed CA1 model, the more distal input configuration showed weaker sensitivity to inhibitory strength within the explored range.

Finally, the study examined a kinematic relation connecting phase correlation, firing frequency, and temporal offset. Its applicability requires approximately periodic firing at a common frequency and a stable output offset, which need not equal the imposed input delay. Overall, the work highlights the combined influence of morphology, active membrane properties, and input location on neuronal synchronization.
